A surgeon's hand shakes a little, even at rest. Pick a hand below and see what each filter does to the signal a surgical robot receives. The learned filter is the real neural network from our project, running live on this page.

Errors are measured against the path the surgeon intended, in micrometres (millionths of a metre), RMS. A human hair is about 70 micrometres thick. These numbers describe the filter's output alone. In the full robot simulation the arm's own controller already smooths some of the tremor, so the end-to-end gain is smaller (about 17% on enhanced tremor; see the results table).

Inside the AI

What the learned filter chooses, moment to moment

Every 20 milliseconds it decides how to blend six signals: five smoothers (heavy to light) and the raw hand. While the needle moves it passes the hand through; while holding it smooths harder.

How to read this
  • Tremor is the fast shake (7 to 12 hertz). Drift is the slow wander. A filter can remove the first; nothing in this lab can remove the second.
  • A fixed low-pass filter smooths the shake but also lags behind real movement. The learned filter picks the smoothing to match what the hand is doing.
  • The speed rule is a hand-written version of the same idea, with no learning.

This lab shows the filter stage only: one axis, simulated tremor, no robot arm. The full closed-loop MuJoCo results, with statistics over 30 paired trials per hand type and 10 trained policies, are in the results table. The tremor is a model, not a recording, and nothing here is clinical.
